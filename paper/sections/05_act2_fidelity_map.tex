\subsection{Attention heads dominate the residual stream}
% finding 05 tabel utama
The best attention-head read-out exceeds the best residual-stream
read-out in every attribute type, and the two largest gains are in types
where the standard read-out was weakest (RELIG$\times$POLPARTY:
$0.26\!\to\!0.60$; RACE$\times$POLPARTY: $0.34\!\to\!0.61$;
Table~\ref{tab:map}). Those are maxima over unequal candidate
sets---1{,}024 heads against 33 layers---and under the symmetric
correction of Sec.~\ref{sec:symmetric} the head advantage holds in five
of six types and reverses in none.
Multi-cue averaging alone already helps (RACE$\times$RELIG residual:
$0.07$ single-template $\to0.27$ \textsc{Tmean}): part of the apparent
weakness was operationalization noise. Figure~\ref{fig:layercurve} shows
this is not a property of one lucky depth: the best head within a layer
sits above that layer's residual stream at nearly every depth in all six
types (the residual stream wins at three isolated layer--type points
out of $186$), and the FFN output tracks the residual stream rather
than the heads. The information the standard read-out misses is thus already
present \emph{before} the per-head signals are projected and summed into
the stream, consistent with dilution at that summation rather than with
the model not encoding it.

\paragraph{Where in the stack, without selection.} Those per-layer curves
take the \emph{best} head at each depth, so they are
selection-contaminated everywhere. Averaging $\rrho$ over all 32 heads of
a layer instead gives a selection-free view of which depths carry the
structure (Table~\ref{tab:layerblock}). One pattern is uniform: the first quarter
of the stack (L0--7) is the weakest block in all six types, so the
geometry is not present at the input end and is built up. The peak,
however, is not uniformly mid-stack---it falls in L8--15 for four types,
in L16--23 for RELIG$\times$POLPARTY, and in the \emph{last} block for
RACE$\times$RELIG, whose block means rise monotonically with depth
($0.09\!\to\!0.17$) and whose best single head sits at L28. The weakest
type is thus also the one whose (marginal) structure emerges latest.

\subsection{Selection, symmetric, and geometry controls}
\label{sec:symmetric}
% finding 06 cek 1-3 + review B1 (residual held-out, proyeksi acak)
Max-statistic permutation tests (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup-selection}): pure
noise allowed to pick its champion among 1{,}024 heads reaches only
$\rrho\approx0.34$--$0.46$ (95th percentile of the null max); the observed
maxima exceed this in all six types (selection-corrected $p<0.0005$ in
four types; $0.0025$ and $0.0155$ in the remaining two). Held-out-template
and split-half estimates agree with each other (Table~\ref{tab:map}),
and head \emph{choice} is stable under resampling for most types
(\starhead{} re-selected in 87/200 splits for RELIG$\times$POLPARTY)
---but is a lottery for RACE$\times$RELIG.

\paragraph{Symmetric correction and a geometry control.} That comparison
is also unequal in dimensionality: 128-dimensional heads might beat the
anisotropic 4{,}096-dimensional stream for geometric rather than
informational reasons. We therefore run one pipeline over three candidate families with
identical held-out-template selection: the 1{,}024 heads, the 33 residual
layers, and 1{,}024 \emph{random 128-dimensional projections} of the
residual stream (32 per layer, fixed across folds;
Appendix~\ref{app:randproj}). Held-out, heads win in five of six
types (e.g., RELIG$\times$POLPARTY: head $0.44$
vs.\ residual $0.17$ vs.\ random projection $0.18$; RACE$\times$RELIG:
$0.29$ vs.\ $0.16$ vs.\ $0.17$); random projections track the residual
stream, not the heads, so the head advantage is not a
dimensionality artifact. The exception is EDUCATION$\times$INCOME, where
the margin is thin ($0.54$ vs.\ $0.51$ vs.\ $0.49$): for that type the
standard read-out is already close to the best we find.

\paragraph{A ceiling for fidelity.} A correlation of $0.63$
means something different under a ceiling of $0.70$ than under $0.95$. We
estimate the ceiling from both sides: the survey RDM's own split-half
reliability (two simulated independent respondent samples per cell at the
real sample sizes) is $0.985$--$0.994$, and the model RDM's reliability
across disjoint template halves at \starhead{} is $0.72$--$0.94$, giving
an attenuation ceiling of $\sqrt{r_{\text{survey}}\,r_{\text{model}}} =
0.84$--$0.96$ per type. Observed fidelity sits at $53$--$74\%$ of that
ceiling for the five stronger types and $39\%$ for RACE$\times$RELIG
(per type in Table~\ref{tab:controls}):
the map is well below what measurement noise alone would allow, so the
unexplained residual is real signal the representation does not carry
(or our read-out does not capture)---not an artifact of a noisy ruler.

\subsection{Not an artifact of the identity words themselves}
\label{sec:lexical}
% review A2: konfound leksikal + partial Spearman. Angka:
% notebooks/output/14_.../lexical_partial.csv (analisis_lokal/lexical_baseline.py)
The prompts differ across cells only in the attribute-value words
(``Democrat'', ``Hindu'', ``30--49'', \dots), and the lexical-semantic
similarity of those words is itself correlated with real opinion
similarity. A fidelity map could in principle be nothing more than a
clean copy of word similarity. A baseline RDM is
built from a small general-purpose sentence encoder
\citep{minilm2020}---in two variants, embedding the value words alone and
embedding the full identity sentence---and correlated with the survey
RDM; we then report the \emph{partial} Spearman correlation of each model
read-out with the survey RDM after controlling for the lexical RDM, with
significance by cell-label permutation.

The confound is real but does not explain the map. The lexical baseline
alone reaches $\rrho=0.21$--$0.64$ against the survey RDM (largest for
EDUCATION$\times$INCOME, where value words are ordered categories).
Controlling for it, \starhead's fidelity remains $\rrho=0.35$--$0.59$
with $p<0.0005$ in five of six types (e.g., AGE$\times$POLPARTY
$0.67\!\to\!0.59$ partial; RELIG$\times$POLPARTY $0.60\!\to\!0.59$;
EDUCATION$\times$INCOME $0.67\!\to\!0.35$, the largest drop, consistent
with its word-order structure). The exception is again
RACE$\times$RELIG: \starhead's partial fidelity there is $0.23$
($p=0.056$; the type's best head survives at $0.41$, $p=0.0015$)---one
more sign that this type's map is marginal, and the fourth control it is
weakest under (Table~\ref{tab:controls}). For word-ordered attribute
types, though, a nontrivial share of the raw correlation is lexical.

\subsection{A general demographic-geometry head}
% finding 06 bonus — klaim bintang
Fixing \starhead{} in advance (no per-type re-selection) yields
significant fidelity in \emph{all six} types
($\rrho=0.33$--$0.67$; permutation $p<0.001$ each, surviving a
conservative $\times1024$ Bonferroni). A single head, one of 1{,}024,
carries a group arrangement that tracks real opinion structure across
age, education, income, religion, party, ideology, and (weakly) race
(Figure~\ref{fig:starhead}). Because the location is fixed before
scoring, this test is structurally immune to the per-type winner's curse
that motivates Sec.~\ref{sec:setup-selection}. One caveat of provenance
remains: \starhead{} was not chosen at random,
it was \emph{noticed} because it recurred in the per-type top-10 lists;
the fixed-location test rules out per-type selection, not the initial
noticing. Independent confirmation therefore has to come from outside
this data: we report a causal test in Sec.~\ref{sec:gap} and a
cross-model replication in Sec.~\ref{sec:crossmodel}, where the
phenomenon recurs at a family-specific address.

\subsection{Heterogeneity is a property of the model, not the read-out}
% finding 02+05: hierarki bertahan; ras rapuh antar template
The type hierarchy is preserved at every location tested:
the age- and education-based types are strongly and stably encoded
(template std $0.02$--$0.05$; RELIG$\times$POLPARTY sits between, at
$0.10$), while every race-containing type is weaker
\emph{and} prompt-fragile (template std $0.12$--$0.13$; e.g.,
RACE$\times$RELIG at its best head: $\rrho=0.16$ under one template,
$0.45$ under another). This parallels output-level findings that
political ideology dominates persona effects \citep{socratic2025} and
annotator-attitude findings that ideological variables outpredict raw
demographics \citep{annotators2022}. Demographic attributes therefore
cannot be treated as interchangeable: a persona pipeline validated on political or
socio-economic attributes gives no warrant for the race-based attributes,
where this model's internal structure is both weaker and unstable under
paraphrase.
